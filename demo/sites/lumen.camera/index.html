Lumen One is the camera we wanted to carry every day. A 40-megapixel full-frame sensor. A 28mm lens, centered by hand. A shutter you can barely hear. All of it in a body machined from a single block of aluminum, small enough to disappear into a coat pocket.

Sensor

Forty megapixels.
Not one wasted.

The new LX-40 back-illuminated sensor holds fifteen stops of dynamic range, so the glare off the river and the shadow in a doorway land in the same frame. A stacked readout clears the full sensor in 1/180 s, which all but ends rolling-shutter skew.

Lens

28mm ƒ/1.7.
Centered by hand, in Porto.

Eleven elements in eight groups, three of them aspherical, ground and coated in our workshop on the Douro. Each lens is individually centered by hand, so it's sharp to the corners wide open, with out-of-focus light that melts instead of buzzing.

Low light

Night mode,
without the mode.

Low light isn't a setting on Lumen One. It's just light. Dual-gain pixels and a six-stop stabilizer let you shoot handheld at half a second, and the noise that's left looks like film grain, not mush.

Small camera.
No small details.

Leaf shutter

So quiet, you'll check that it fired.

A 1/4000 s leaf shutter syncs flash at every speed and makes almost no sound at all. Street photographers, rejoice quietly.
